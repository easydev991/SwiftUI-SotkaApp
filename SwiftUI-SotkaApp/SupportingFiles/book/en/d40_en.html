Day 40.

Day 40. Develop your potential 100%!





There's too much speculation about genetics around us, and too many people excuse themselves by saying they just don't have very good genes. At one time people told us you can't get built on the bars like the black guys in the videos because white people have different genetics. And so on. Usually that was said by people who were far from being in their best shape themselves. But that, apparently, was genetics too.

Of course there are a number of parameters that depend on genetics and that we inherit (for example, the ratio of fast and slow fibers in your body, how efficiently the nervous system works, the length of muscles and tendons, and so on). Some of them we can't change no matter how hard we try, others we can if we work.

But you know what? In the end it doesn't decide whether you'll succeed or not. Treat your genetics as starting conditions. Some people have better ones, some have worse ones, but the one who works more always wins!



We get it when athletes or professional competitors talk about genetics. There a difference of fractions of a second really can be explained by one person's genetic predisposition and another person's not-so-great predisposition. Or a person was simply built for that specific sport — Michael Phelps, for example, whose foot structure genetically helps him swim. But you should understand that those people are competing with each other at the limit of human ability. When all the ordinary tools are already used up. When all the advanced steroid technologies are already in play. Then they reach the limit, and the one whose limit is farther wins.

But we're not planning to become Olympic champions, are we? Doesn't seem like it. We all just want to get ourselves into great shape. And that's available to everyone. Anyone can get better simply by starting to train and watching what they eat. And anyone can get much better if over time they train smarter and harder and take nutrition more seriously. For some people it will take more time, for others less, but in the end the result will come to everyone.

We'll tell you a short story now. This story is from Dale Carnegie's book "How to Stop Worrying and Start Living." And it's a lesson.

Dale Carnegie:
Once I visited a happy farmer at his home in Florida. When he first bought the farm, he was driven to despair.

The land turned out to be good for nothing. You couldn't grow fruit on it or raise pigs. The only things that grew on the plot were holly oaks, and it was full of rattlesnakes. But eventually it hit him. What if he turned the minuses into pluses? Why not get something useful out of the rattlesnakes? To everyone's surprise, he started canning rattlesnake meat. Several years ago I stayed at his farm and learned that crowds of tourists were eager to see this rattlesnake farm. Twenty thousand people visited it every year. His business was thriving. The venom from the snakes' fangs was sent to laboratories and used to make antitoxin serum. The snakeskins sold for fabulous money. Women's shoes and handbags were made from them. I saw canned rattlesnake meat being sent to customers all over the world. I bought a postcard with a view of the farm and mailed it from the post office of a village that had been renamed "Rattlesnake," in honor of the man who turned a poisonous lemon into sweet lemonade.


If anyone didn't get the last sentence, there's a saying: "If life hands you a lemon, make lemonade!" That applies to genetics too. Too many people have started calling their laziness genetics, instead of just going and starting to change their life.
