Day 9. How to breathe during training?





Everyone knows that during training it's very important to breathe correctly. Unfortunately a lot of people, especially beginners, forget about it as soon as they start training hard, and breathe however it happens — and sometimes they even hold their breath under load. At the same time, correct breathing, synced with the exercises, will let you raise the effectiveness of your workouts a lot, and also improve your results.

In general form, the breathing rules for exercises look like this:

1. Take a breath before you start the exercise (and each rep in it).
2. At the same time as the muscles contract (the concentric phase), exhale.
3. At the same time as the muscles relax (the eccentric phase), inhale.

Let's look at correct breathing using pull-ups as an example.

You walk up to the bar, put your hands on it. You inhale. You start contracting the arm and back muscles, pull yourself up, and at the same time exhale (ideally, at the top you make a full exhale). Then, smoothly relaxing the muscles, you straighten the arms, lower the body down, and smoothly inhale (ideally, at the bottom you take a deep inhale).

The depth of inhales and exhales should be comfortable, which depends directly on the tempo of the exercise (we'll talk about that in a separate infopost). Keep in mind that a high exercise tempo makes you breathe faster and more often, which means with each inhale you only get a small volume of air. That's important to remember so you don't lose your breath and "suffocate".

By the way, about the recommendation to breathe through the nose. There are 2 main reasons for that:

1. This way the stream of cold air is warmed, without which the brain and nasopharynx would be overcooled, and that warm air is kept (relevant in the cold season);
2. The mucous membrane and hairs filter the air to screen out dust and microbes;

At the same time, if you exhale through the mouth, you can let the air out faster that way.

It's also worth noting that correct breathing depends on what type of exercises you're doing.

When doing strength exercises — and that's exactly what we do in the SOTKA program — it's recommended to exhale at the moment of greatest muscular effort, and inhale when muscle tension is the smallest. As you let air out of the lungs, the diaphragm and abs tense slightly (you can notice how the belly pulls in a bit on the exhale). On the inhale it's the opposite: the diaphragm and abs relax (the belly sticks out a bit).

Always remember that during training you must not hold your breath. The thing is that during an inhale, intra-abdominal and blood pressure rise and the load on the cardiovascular system increases. Adding the load of the movement on top of that is clearly not worth it. Plus it's easier to concentrate the effort on the exhale. So if you notice you're starting to hold your breath, that means the exercise is too hard for you, and you should lower the intensity of the load.

When doing flexibility exercises, which we do at the end of each training week, remember the main rule: relaxation (stretching) of the muscles is done on the exhale, and returning to the starting position — on the inhale.

When doing cyclic (aerobic) exercises such as walking, running, cycling, swimming, and so on, it's very important to keep an even breathing rhythm that matches the intensity of the movements.

Jerky breathing with constant holds, or, conversely, breathing that's too frequent, breaks the even rhythm of the movements, makes it harder for the body to coordinate them, and doesn't give the lungs enough ventilation.
Protein requirement (‘Number of muscles, which is renewed in 1 day’ x ‘% of protein in muscles’) = 54 grams


Thus, a person weighing 100kg with 50% of the muscles in the body needs to get 54 grams of protein per day to maintain (!) normal functioning of the current number of muscles.

There are three important things to be said:

1. We talk about maintenance of normal functioning of the available muscular volume, so, in the meaning of usual activity. If a person starts to train actively, his protein needs increase, because the body needs to build new muscles.

2. These calculations take into account the need for protein only for muscles, although, as you already know, proteins are necessary for all tissues in the body without exception as a building material.

3. If your goal is to gain muscle mass and you want to know how much protein you need to add to your diet, then you need to calculate the protein requirement for the new weight and subtract the current protein requirement from it.
