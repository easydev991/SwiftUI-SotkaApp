Day 45. Strengthening immunity





Immunity is our body's ability to resist foreign antigenic agents, and how good that ability is depends directly on what we do or don't do. We've tried to explain the essence of this question clearly and simply in today's infopost!

Theory


At the molecular level, antigenic properties belong first of all to proteins and nucleic acids, but also to polysaccharides, lipopolysaccharides, polypeptides, and some artificial high-polymer compounds. There is no organism that doesn't include at least a few of the molecules listed — even viruses are made of proteins and nucleic acids (and sometimes polysaccharides), so this defense works "against everyone."

Antigens are like fingerprints, and it's by them that the immune system recognizes "self" and "not self." Let's figure out what the body can react to:

1. To the substances listed above on their own. If foreign antigens enter the body, it reacts to them — it always reacts — and that protects it. In most cases you can't notice these reactions, but sometimes they make themselves known.

2. To various infectious agents (viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa, helminths) and the products of their activity.

3. Foreign and dangerous cells that appeared in the body itself. Every day, mutant and tumor cells form in each of us, and the body destroys them quite effectively. If something fails, cancer develops.

4. To pharmaceuticals, food, cosmetics, clothes (!), and other things around us that our body can come into contact with.

5. To some kinds of plastic, metals, coatings, and chemicals.

6. To its own organs and tissues. That kind of intolerance toward itself can appear for different reasons, and the condition is called autoimmune pathology.

We should also mention that our immunity has a "memory" — the ability to remember antigens it meets in life, so that on a repeat encounter it can react faster and more effectively. That's what makes it possible to create vaccines against some diseases. But it's worth noting that some antigens the body can't remember at all, some it remembers for several years, and some — practically for life.

Another feature is tied to that. To all the substances listed above, immunity reacts poorly on the first encounter, and well already on the second or third. You need to take that into account with allergic reactions — if the first time an allergy may show up as a small itchy spot, the second time the body can react "all the way" — with Quincke's edema or anaphylactic shock. And that's why the phrase "I never used to be allergic to this substance" has its meaning: there wasn't any before, and then it appeared, on the second or third contact.

Practice


Now let's go through methods that can help you get sick less often and feel better. We'll try to put them in a "hit parade" of importance, although that's very relative. The main thing is the cumulative effect that appears if you follow as many points from this list as you can.

Nutrition




The first thing to think about, if we're talking about global immunity problems in the population, is: does this population eat well?

An adult's immune system weighs about 1 kg in total. A huge part of it is cells that constantly divide and renew, and that requires a lot of… no, not vitamins — PROTEIN! If there's a deficit, especially of an amino acid like glutamine, the countless warriors of the immune system — leukocytes, lymphocytes, eosinophils, macrophages, and so on — won't be able to multiply normally.

Besides that, the whole biological defense system is one solid mass of proteins. Can this system exist without adequate nutrition, especially in the case of a protein deficit? (hint: it can't).

Besides proteins, the immune system, which works hard every minute and every hour, needs a lot of other nutrients and energy: carbs, polyunsaturated fatty acids, and so on. So the first enemy of the immune system is protein-calorie insufficiency and irrational nutrition.

Reducing contact with antigens




Constant contact with antigens spreads the "attention" of the immune system thin. Besides bacteria, viruses, and other "aggressors," the body has to come into contact every day with an incredible number of other antigens: food, clothes, pharmaceuticals, perfume, detergents and cosmetics, pet hair — an incredible amount of everything.

You need to build proper hygiene habits that will help you lower the load on the immune system. The important thing here is not to fall into paranoia and try to live in sterile conditions, talking to the world through a hypoallergenic spacesuit — without some level of stimulation the immune system will start to wither too.

Obviously you need to wash your hands, wash your body, change clothes on time, and follow other simple hygiene rules. You need to sanitize (treat) all possible sources of chronic infection: cavities, inflammatory skin diseases, gut infections, fungus on the feet and nails, and so on — all of that lowers your immunity, because it has to constantly fight battles where it doesn't need to. It's also a good idea to minimize contact with sick people.

You need to watch your reaction to foods and other allergens, and avoid contact with them. If you're allergic to a pet, and at the same time you constantly catch colds, then as long as it lives in your home, even if you eat so much garlic you turn into Korean carrot salad, you won't stop getting sick.

Weight control




Both excess and insufficient weight show a consistent negative effect on the immune system. People with obesity have a higher risk of infectious diseases, blood infections, various skin diseases, and their wounds heal poorly. A sharp loss of body weight, and also a constant deficit of it, is also a risk of complications on the immune-system side.

Hardening and other wellness practices




Hardening is stress. Any stress makes your body "lose it" and feel a threat to its well-being. Any living organism then starts to adjust — to widen the borders of its capabilities and reserves.

But you should take into account that if you suddenly and too strongly stress the body, all the defense systems will be suppressed. The same thing happens when the stress is chronic and the body doesn't have time to adapt to it (it doesn't matter whether it's psychological stress or constant cold). Self-regulation mechanisms switch on that rebuild all the body's systems into a new format of work — and often the body remembers old ailments.

Physical activity




Sport stimulates immunity and the body's resistance by roughly the same mechanism as hardening, plus a number of physiological effects: the cardiovascular system works better, which "pumps the blood" better and matters for immunity; hormonal self-regulation happens; metabolic activity rises; the gut empties better and various pathological processes in it decrease — and so on, you can't list all the effects.

You should take into account that large and especially extreme physical loads lower immunity, so if you plan to push a session until you lose your pulse, try not to get too cold and give yourself stronger nutrition, including protein and carbs after training.

Healthy sleep




We've already written about this in a separate infopost, but we still want to repeat and remind you that full, healthy sleep is very important for keeping immunity high, while various kinds of sleep debt are stress for the body and affect it negatively.

Emotional stability




Emotional swings and stress reliably lower immunity, and they also throw all the body's systems out of balance and disrupt how they work. So take care to keep your feet warm and your head cool (in that saying, "cool" is meant figuratively).

Everything that raises your mood: music, reading books, humor, a positive take on life — also raises your immunity.

Onion, garlic, and other phytoncides




This folk remedy can be counted as effective. I don't know what plays a bigger role — the phytoncides that kill bacteria, or the biologically active substances that affect the body — but eating them from time to time will improve how your immune system works. Just don't forget that stale breath is extremely bad for being understood.

Pharmacy products




If we're talking about various plant immunomodulators (extracts of ginseng, schisandra, eleutherococcus), their effectiveness is usually not very high. Any other immunomodulators and stimulants — only when indicated and after a consult with a competent doctor.

Summer trips to the sea




Resort therapy, especially by the sea (salty air), has an extremely beneficial effect on the whole body, including the immune system (but not for everyone — some people need a different kind of rest). Keep in mind that the effect only comes if you stay at the resort for at least 2 weeks (acclimatization takes about 7–10 days), and better 3–4 weeks in a row.

If you can't get to the sea, get an inexpensive voucher to a rest base in a pine forest, on a riverbank (moderate time in the sun also plays a big role), or somewhere else — rest for 2–3 weeks 1–2 times a year is very good for health.

Everything else


Everything else you can think of (as long as you follow at least the first 4 points), if you put it together, can be equaled in effect to one point.

All those yogurts, lactobacteria (without a real reason), noni juices, vitamins in capsules and tablets, salt lamps and silver balls, eucalyptus candles and "living water" — all together they can make at most one contribution to the piggy bank of your health.

We'll repeat: if you gather all-all-all of them into one pile, they're unlikely to be as effective as one full, rational diet.

Is it worth spending money on all that if there are simple, proven methods whose combination will give you the maximum of what you can get? That's for you to decide.

P.S. Be reasonable. Everything described is guaranteed to fit healthy people, although it also fits most sick people no less. But if you're 60, you have obesity, angina, and hypertension, and after reading this text you suddenly start losing weight, running, drinking schisandra, plunging into a bath, and stuffing yourself with onion, you're more likely to get hit by a stroke than to strengthen your immunity.
