The menstrual cycle and physical activity


The menstrual cycle is a biological process that prepares a woman for pregnancy. If fertilization does not happen, the mucous lining of the uterus with the matured egg is rejected, which shows up as bleeding. At the same time the functional state of the nervous, cardiovascular, endocrine and other systems also changes.

In this article we will focus on two directions:

1. Processes that happen in the female body in different periods of the monthly cycle, and how those processes connect with the choice of physical load (we look at changes in the body of a woman of reproductive age who is not taking oral hormonal contraceptives and is not in menopause);

2. A list of recommended and undesirable exercises during menstruation.

The average cycle is considered to be 28 days. It has a certain hormonal rhythm. The monthly cycle can be divided into 5 stages:

1. Menstrual (duration 3–5 days, days from the start of the cycle 1–5) — contractions of the uterus can cause pain of different intensity, and the hormonal shift connected with a pregnancy that did not happen leads to a vascular spasm, against which a woman can feel general weakness and headaches. During the period there are hormonal swings. The amount of estrogen rises, the concentration of progesterone falls. So the musculature relaxes, and the level of physical capacity drops. In this period it is reasonable to develop flexibility;

2. Postmenstrual (duration 7–9 days, days from the start of the cycle 6–12) — the level of estrogens in the blood is unusually high in this period. That is exactly why there is a sudden surge of strength and incredible work capacity. This is a favorable time to develop endurance. You can include any strength exercises with bodyweight, dumbbells or a barbell, but fast reactions are difficult.

3. Ovulatory (duration 2–4 days, days from the start of the cycle 13–15) — in this period work capacity is at its lowest;

4. Postovulatory (duration 7–9 days, days from the start of the cycle 16–24) — the hormone progesterone is actively produced, preparing the woman's body for fertilization. This is a phase of raised work capacity. Because the body is at the peak of its activity, training can include any dynamic sports aimed at developing speed and strength;

5. Premenstrual (duration 3–5 days, days from the start of the cycle 25–28) — the period is characterized by a significant drop in progesterone and estrogen. Speed of performing an exercise, as well as speed and strength endurance, get worse.

So, knowing the features of our body, we can pick the most effective training programs and sessions, and form different load options without causing discomfort to the body.

Girls and women who start the SOTKA program can face this situation: inside this educational program certain basic exercises are done every day, and over time they become more varied and more complex. The feature is that the exercises are done every day, 5 days a week, and only one day is given for rest and one day for stretching. In this well-thought-out system there was no special place for such a "female event" as the menstrual cycle. But this program would not be itself if it did not create special options and opportunities for women. Without much extra effort, using the information above about hormonal rhythms, you can easily distribute the load. For example: by combining 2 weeks you can create 4 days that do not contain heavy physical activity, and you can use those days during the first (menstrual) stage — rest, rest, yoga, Pilates, cardio, sport walking, stretching, and so on. In this kind of combination many options open up. But the decision to do the exercises or to drop them for a while depends on how the girl feels and on her fitness level. Only that is the key indicator and the deciding argument for or against sports training during the period.

By applying simple knowledge about how the female body works and listening to elementary advice, you can safely do sports, support and ease how you feel, and create a comfortable state for the body.
