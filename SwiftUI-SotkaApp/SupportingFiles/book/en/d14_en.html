Street Workout is something much bigger than just bodyweight training at outdoor sports playgrounds. It's a lifestyle, and we want to introduce you to it as fully as possible. That's why on the last day of the training week our infoposts will talk not only about training or nutrition. For example, today we'll talk about the 10,000-hour rule!

According to research by sociologist Anders Ericsson, it takes about 10,000 hours of practice to reach world-class level. And although the research was done on musicians (first violinists, then pianists), he concludes that this rule is true for any area of human activity. Music (the legendary Beatles are also given as an example), intellectual games (chess and Fischer), business (Bill Gates). Doesn't matter. 10,000 hours of practice, and success is almost guaranteed.

Even though the rule itself (especially the rigid figure of 10,000 hours) can be questioned, even simple common sense confirms the idea. The more you do something, the better you become at it. Especially if you understand what you're doing and try to improve, rather than just repeating the same thing day after day.

It takes a certain amount of time to learn from scratch how to do even basic exercises correctly — push-ups, pull-ups, and squats. That's why our program is built on a circuit principle. That's why we focus our efforts on learning correct technique for these exercises in 100 days. You need to learn to play scales before you start playing pieces, rhythms, dynamics, intonation, and so on.

Besides there being a certain overall-quantity barrier you need to get past, you should also remember that our body has thresholds for the minimum and maximum number of training sessions a day, below which the body won't take the information as significant (by analogy with insufficient load) or above which it won't be able to take further training because of fatigue and a drop in concentration. We built the SOTKA program with this principle in mind, picking a load so you can train every day and not overdo it. By the way, those who stick to the original 1-2-2-2 scheme will do several thousand pull-ups, push-ups, squats, and lunges by the end of the program (that exercise will appear in your arsenal soon)! That's already quite enough to develop correct technique — provided you listen carefully to our advice and watch the quality of every exercise!

Unfortunately a lot of people want everything at once, and so they start chasing skills or try to learn harder exercise variations before they're ready. No need to rush — you're planning to train your whole life, so a couple of months or even years don't play a special role if you really like training.

It's quite possible that the most effective training option, from the point of view of building technique, would be absolutely identical circuits through the whole program. But we also understand perfectly well that this is not only about physical load, but also about emotional load. And that's why adding variety to training has a positive effect on participants (especially those who start from absolute zero). So, while constantly repeating how important correct technique is, from time to time we'll modify your training program, making it more interesting and harder.
