Day 8.

Day 8. Correct exercise technique




Let's start with the most important thing. There is no ideal form for doing exercises. Period. Someone will consider one way of doing them correct, someone else another; CrossFitters even consider their kipping pull-ups no worse than regular ones (which depresses me, but that's a separate conversation).

The point is that any exercise can have a huge number of variations. You can place the hands a bit differently, you can shift body position, you can do them in different ranges of motion, and so on. And a person is free to do it the way they consider necessary and correct — that's their business.

When we talk about exercise form within the SOTKA program, we talk about the need to meet the following 2 conditions:

1. the form of the exercise should be as safe as possible
2. the form of the exercise should be as effective as possible

It's from the standpoint of these conditions that we comment on technique.

We don't think we need to explain why we care so much about safety. Nobody likes getting injured, especially beginners, because they have so much drive to train, and sometimes that drive is hard to hold back. So one of the first things we want to teach is safe training. And that's only possible if you do the exercises in a safe form.

But safety by itself is only half the job. The second point that determines our approach to choosing exercise form is directly tied to how effective that form is for the goals we set. If doing push-ups through a full range of motion is safer and more effective than doing them through half the range, we'll suggest the first option. And that applies to all the exercises and techniques we talk about. We will never put effectiveness above safety, because in the long run what matters to us is not so much the instant result as our own health and its steady development.

We put the emphasis on these two conditions; in everything else we give full freedom of action and even encourage you to learn to better understand your body and how it works. We're all different, so there is no single ideal method that fits everyone, or one exercise that will give everyone the same results. Everything is individual and learned by trial and error — and so those trials don't lead to unpleasant consequences, always remember that exercises need to be done safely and effectively.
