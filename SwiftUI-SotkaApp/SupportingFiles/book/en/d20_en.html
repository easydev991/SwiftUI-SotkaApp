Day 20. Building a diet (practice)





The quintessence of this week's infoposts will be a practical guide to building a diet on your own. Get ready for there being a lot of text, but we tried to make it as simple and clear as possible.

Diet-building scheme


1. Count the calorie content of the current diet.

2. Check balance by PFC and minerals.

3. Check the quality of the products.

4. Determine the direction of movement (weight gain, weight maintenance, weight loss).

5. Make adjustments to the diet, keeping balance and quality.

6. After 4–6 weeks, assess the results.

Calorie content of the current diet


To understand which direction to move, you first need to determine where you are now. For that you need to determine the number of calories consumed a day at present. What's important is not a specific value on a specific day, but the average value over 1–2 weeks.

For that you need to start writing down everything that's eaten through the day. You can write it on paper, in Excel, in some app, or on a specialized site. It's important to write down absolutely everything, because (and this is proven by studies) people are very strongly mistaken (in the direction of underestimating) about how many calories they actually consume.

It's worth noting that there are many apps and sites where a large product database is already collected and constantly updated, and you can also create your own recipes and add your own products to your personal database.

We want to draw attention once more that during cooking the weight of products can change and this will affect the calorie content of the dish in different ways (ideally you should write down the weight of all the pots in the house — that will save some hassle).

Cooking methods and calorie content:
Different cooking methods affect products differently. For example, when frying on vegetable oil products such as meat, meat offal, fish, you need to add 20% of the total calorie content of the oil you poured into the pan. That is, if you pour 100 g of oil into the pan when frying meat, about 20 g will soak into the meat, another part will stay in the pan, and another part will evaporate. Of course it's better not to fry on oil at all, and to steam or boil, but not everyone can give up oil.

When boiling meat and fish, 20% of its raw calorie content boils out into the broth. So we subtract 20%.

If we bake products in the oven or microwave, the calorie content stays the same.

The calorie content of grains and pasta is stated for the dry product. When cooked in water (which has 0 calories) they swell and increase in volume. It turns out the volume and weight increased, but the calorie content stayed the same. And that's a fairly common beginner mistake, when, for example, they cook 150 grams of rice, getting about 800 grams cooked (conditionally), and count the calorie content from those 800.


When counting calories the hardest part is lasting the first week. A lot of people can't fit all these numbers in their head and in the end even give up on counting. And those who get into it quite quickly create their own product database, and further counting takes them no more than a couple of minutes a day. And since most people's diet is the same and is cooked by the same recipes, it will be enough to count everything once.

A tip for the lazy:
To avoid counting the calorie content of a separate portion every time, you can use various containers, for example glass containers or cups, as a portion-size determiner. Then it will be enough to count the calorie content of the container once and further measure everything in containers.


Balance of the diet


After the calorie content and composition of the diet are determined, the first thing you should do is check it for balance. In previous posts we already wrote that WHO recommends sticking to the following ratio: proteins — 10–15%, fats — 15–30% (including essential fatty acids such as Omega-3 and Omega-6), carbs — 55–75%. This ratio is given by calorie content. But it's easier to weigh in grams, so if you convert to grams, remember that the calorie content of 1 gram of fat is more than twice as high (9 versus 4) as proteins and carbs. In the end you'll get something like: proteins — 11–18%, fats — 7–16%, carbs — 66–82%.

We want to remind you once more that the ratio above is NOT dogma, and it can differ, but that's what you can start from. Especially in terms of normalizing Fats and Carbs, and we've already talked about the role of all the "materials" for our body.

You also need to check that fiber is present in the diet in a volume of 25–30 grams (the recommended daily norm for an adult). By itself it isn't digested, but it stimulates intestinal motility.

Product quality


After the diet is balanced by macronutrients, you need to make sure that all the "materials" come from quality sources. Because with quality products it's much harder to "overdo" calorie content than with all kinds of cakes and sodas.

For convenience we've already compiled a list of such sources and even split it into categories. Use it in good health!

Protein products




Poultry:
A variety of tastes and the relatively low cost of basic options (chicken, turkey, goose) — that's what makes poultry a good protein source. Besides protein, poultry is rich in microelements, for example iron and zinc. Keep in mind that poultry meat differs a lot in fat content. Choose less fatty kinds of meat, separate the skin and visible fat. As a cooking method you should prefer stewing, boiling, and baking. For taste variety you can include grilled poultry in the diet a few times a month. Chicken and turkey breasts are a lean protein source with very low fat. That's especially relevant if the goal is fat loss.


Eggs:
A good amino-acid profile, together with the vitamins and minerals in them, also make eggs a good protein source. There are varieties additionally enriched with essential omega-3 fatty acids, which makes them even more useful. The egg yolk contains quite a lot of fat, and also cholesterol, which people with a predisposition to cardiovascular disease should take into account. They're recommended to limit consumption of whole eggs to one a day. Eggs should be consumed in thermally processed form.


Red meat:
Various kinds of red meat (beef, lamb, veal, pork) are also distinguished by a good amino-acid profile, a high content of vitamins, creatine, microelements such as zinc and iron, and conjugated linoleic acid. Offal is especially distinguished by a high content of vitamins and microelements. For consumption you should choose lean kinds of meat that have been boiled, stewed, or baked.


Fish and seafood:
A high-quality protein source with excellent taste. Lean kinds of fish contain a large amount of protein and are distinguished by low calorie content. At the same time most fatty kinds are distinguished by a high level of useful fats, in particular essential omega-3 fatty acids. Alternating different kinds of fish not only lowers diet boredom, it also makes it more useful. It should be noted that sea fish (especially large predators) can contain a large amount of methylmercury, in connection with which pregnant women and small children should limit or exclude its consumption. But if you study tables of mercury content in fish, you can pick a safe option. Seafood — squid, crabs, shrimp, mussels, rapana, krill, and others — are lean and tasty protein sources.


Fermented dairy products:
Low-fat (3–5%) cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, and also ricotta, brynza, and other cheeses (within 3–7% fat) will enrich the diet not only with protein but also with calcium. These products let you vary the taste of food, because they can be consumed in various forms: sweet, salty, without additives, in combination with vegetables, fruit, and other products. Fattier kinds of cheese are very calorie-dense, and you should watch their consumption. Protein shakes made from milk powder, egg white, or soy, protein shakes — are a decent source of dietary protein with a complete composition and various tastes and are convenient to use. But you shouldn't overestimate this food product — it's more of a food supplement than a full food replacement, but there's also no reason to avoid it.


Legumes:
The protein-product group can also include legumes, despite the high carb content in them. Soy and, accordingly, soy products also belong to legumes. Despite some warnings about phytoestrogens in them, there are no reasons to fully refuse this group of products — in a serving of any soy products the content of these substances doesn't carry health risks. Peanuts, because of high fat content, should be rationed the same as other nuts (see "Sources of useful fats").


Complex carbs




Porridges:
Buckwheat, oatmeal, pearl barley, barley porridge, rice of various kinds — tasty and useful complex carb sources. At moderate calorie content they include a large amount of useful substances, including vitamins and fiber.


Products from durum wheat:
Products from durum wheat (pasta, spaghetti, ravioli) became popular because they satiate well and give good appetite control, digesting for up to 8 h.


Bread:
Bread is an inseparable part of most traditional and modern food cultures. But you should keep in mind not only the rich composition of this product, but also the fairly high calorie content; this is especially necessary to take into account when losing weight. Prefer coarse-ground bakery products, rye bread, lean options like lavash. White bread and baguettes are not excluded, but if the goal is fat loss, their amount needs to be limited.


Potatoes:
Potatoes are rich in useful minerals, and when cooked without adding fat they have fairly moderate calorie content, so they carry no threats to the figure if they fit into a correct diet scheme.


Soups:
Soups can't be unambiguously called a carb product, because very often the broth contains a lot of fat. At the same time a serving of lean soup is about equal in calorie content to a serving of other carbs. So if you want soups in your diet, you can cook them several times a week.


Fats




Useful:
Sources of so-called "useful" fats include vegetable oils (olive, flaxseed, rapeseed, and others), nuts, dried seeds (after thermal processing useful fats lose their properties), olives, avocado, and also fish oil in capsules. A sufficient amount of fats in the diet is necessary for normal functioning of the endocrine system, good mood and well-being, health of skin, hair, and nails, and many other body functions.


Keep in mind that an excess of any fats and calories will not bring you positive results and can have a negative effect on health in the distant future. Ration consumption of the listed products. In one day you need to consume either 15–20 g of vegetable oils, adding them to salads or other products, or 30–50 g of dried nuts and seeds, or as a food additive you can use olives and avocado. You can also consume fish oil in capsules according to the instructions for use (after consulting a doctor).

Vegetables and fruit




Vegetables and fruit are distinguished by a rich mineral composition, vitamins, biologically active substances such as fruit acids, pectins, gums, and so on. An important component is fiber. Modern city residents consume it 2–4 times less than the norm, and that raises the risk of developing cardiovascular and oncological diseases and disrupts the digestive system.

Vegetables:
Raw vegetables and greens, mushrooms, corn, canned vegetables (green peas), processed vegetables (cauliflower, broccoli, zucchini, turnip), green beans, carrots, cabbage, radish, green onion, seaweed, dill, parsley, spinach, arugula, sorrel, and others. Try to consume plant food of different colors. That will let you expand the selection of nutrient varieties in the diet. Prefer raw plant products, especially leafy green vegetables, but don't ignore thermally processed vegetables and fruit and even pickles in moderate amounts. Include more seasonal products in the diet; they often have higher taste qualities and are much cheaper.


Fruit and berries:
Fruit: apple, pomegranate, peach, pineapple, pear, orange, banana, kiwi, grapefruit, tangerine, mango, melon, and so on.

Berries: strawberry, raspberry, currant, blueberry, sweet cherry, cherry, gooseberry, watermelon.


Most expert organizations in health, including the World Health Organization (WHO), recommend increasing consumption of vegetables and fruit to 5–8 servings a day and note that this has a positive effect on health and lifespan.

Drinks




Sufficient hydration is an important condition of health and good well-being. A wide range of drinks is used to quench thirst, each of which has both advantages and disadvantages.

A number of drinks contain extra calories. That's their main minus — consuming several glasses a day is equal in calorie content to a serving of solid food. At the same time liquid calories practically don't satiate, and such drinks contain fewer useful nutrients. That's important to take into account if you want to lose weight. In that case liquid calories should be avoided. At the same time their drawback can turn into an advantage when gaining weight. If you can't get weight off a dead stop, liquid calories will help raise the calorie content of the diet without overloading the digestive system.

Juices, fruit drinks, compotes, lemonades:
They contain sugar, both natural and refined. The positive in this group of drinks is a pleasant taste. They often contain vitamins and microelements, especially freshly squeezed juices. This group of drinks quenches thirst well and helps avoid dehydration at a very high air temperature.


Milk and liquid dairy products:
A good source of calcium and protein. You should take into account the fat content in these products, especially those who easily gain extra weight.


Coffee and tea:
Caffeine-containing products such as tea, coffee, and mate are rich in minerals, vitamins, and biologically active substances and are valued for their taste. At the same time it's not rational to consume them in too large amounts — a couple or three cups a day is enough.

Herbal teas (mint tea, hibiscus, and others) are suitable for expanding taste variety and don't contain caffeine.

You can add sugar to tea and coffee; in that case they become calorie drinks with the properties described above. If you like a sweet taste but don't want to consume extra calories, you can use sugar substitutes.


Soda:
Carbonated drinks contain dissolved carbonic acid, due to which they shift the acid–alkaline balance in the body toward the acid side. With constant consumption this can disrupt the acid–alkaline balance and have a negative effect, for example, on bone-tissue health. At the same time there's no reason to exclude them fully.


Determining direction


At the very beginning of this week we already discussed the calorie-balance concept, and we strongly recommend starting to deal with your diet exactly from the so-called "balanced scheme".

If weight is stable, calories in, overall, equal calories out. If there's no goal to change the current weight, you can eat by this scheme, in parallel doing sport and gradually improving various characteristics of the body. You'll invariably return to this same scheme after fat-loss or mass-gain cycles, on which there's a clearly expressed calorie deficit or surplus.

If weight is dropping, there's a calorie deficit. Depending on the goal, you can either correct calorie content by increasing portion size, or leave everything as is if you want to lose weight and are comfortable on the current scheme.

If weight is increasing, there's a calorie surplus. The correction principles don't differ: you need to regulate portion size, choosing less fatty food. If you have experience following irrational diets for a long time, you don't need to rush to sharply correct your diet. It's better to wait for weight to stabilize than to fall into the vicious circle of incorrect fat loss again.

Making adjustments to the diet


We suggest splitting the daily diet into 3 main meals (+ a snack), each of which, in turn, will consist of 4 parts, one for each product category — protein, complex carbs, salads, water (drinks).

The size of each part (portion) is roughly equal to the size of a fist. That's convenient, because it always lets you have a way to determine portion size at hand. Besides that, the fist is proportional to body size, so some individualization is also built into this parameter. For owners of too-large or too-small fists, portion size can be increased/decreased by 10–15%. You don't need to be afraid of making a mistake or some measurement error here, because they won't have a substantial effect on the final result.

If you want more precise numbers, for that you need to weigh a portion of a protein dish or fruit. For women its weight on average is 140–160 grams, for men — 200–220 grams or a bit more (but not less). For more precise diet tuning you can from time to time count the calorie content and composition of the resulting menu, but often there's no need for that.

To start, you should try eating at equal intervals and gradually pick the most convenient option. You can also change the order of eating portions in the scheme and even split the diet into a larger number of meals, for example split fruit into several intakes. But before changing something, we recommend trying the standard scheme — maybe it will fit best.

By the way, in this eating scheme there are no limits on choosing a time for a meal. For example, if you like a late dinner, that's acceptable, although it's desirable to have dinner no later than 1.5–2 hours before sleep.

For 3–4 weeks you need to follow the eating scheme, controlling weight dynamics. Also remember that weight can change through the day, so you need to weigh yourself at the same time and look at the average weight over several days.

An example of building a diet


In the following example we'll show all the steps described above as they're actually done. With real numbers and products!

1. We counted the calorie content of the current diet. It came out to 2000 calories.
2. We determined the goal. We decided to lose a bit of weight. We subtracted 10% from the calorie content. The calorie content of the target diet came out to 1800 calories.
3. We distributed by Proteins, Fats, and Carbs, going by WHO recommendations, for example 20% protein (360 calories), 20% fats (360 calories), 60% carbs (1080 calories).
4. We converted to grams for convenience of counting when cooking, because on products the composition is written in grams. We got — 90 grams of Protein, 40 grams of Fat, 270 grams of Carbs.
5. We start shuffling our usual diet so that it fits the needed PFC limits. Yes, it won't work right away, but after several iterations and rearrangements everything will come together. Preferences for meal sizes are individual; we take a case where calorie content is roughly the same in each of them.

Breakfast:

1. Muesli (40 g) with milk (200 ml)

2. Tea with sugar (16 g) and cottage cheese 8% fat (100 g)

C/P/F/C: C — 436 kcal, P — 23 g, F — 16 g, C — 50 g

Lunch:

1. Buckwheat porridge (150 g) with boiled chicken liver (80 g)

2. Radish (100 g) and sweet bell pepper (150 g)

3. Wheat bread (30 g)

4. Tea with sugar (16 g) and cottage cheese 8% fat (100 g)

C/P/F/C: C — 602 kcal, P — 46 g, F — 14 g, C — 73 g

Snack:

Apple — 150 g

C/P/F/C: C — 70 kcal, P — 1 g, F — 1 g, C — 14 g

Dinner:

1. Pilaf (200 g)

2. Wheat bread (35 g)

3. Fresh cucumbers (150 g)

4. Tea with sugar (16 g) with bread (35 g) and honey (40 g)

C/P/F/C: C — 671 kcal, P — 19 g, F — 7 g, C — 133 g

Total: C — 1778 kcal, P — 89 g, F — 38 g, C — 270 g

6. This is an example of a diet for one or several identical days. A diet like this should be compiled for periods of 1–2 weeks, watching product variety. This will take a lot of time only the first time. Then you can use the resulting scheme and not think about what to cook on a given day for a specific meal.

Conclusion


With the right selection of all the parameters listed above you can lose weight (or gain mass) comfortably and steadily, reducing the amount of fat tissue but keeping muscle and health as a whole. Mistakes in balancing nutrients and selecting calorie content can lead to feeling unwell and even to illnesses!

And the main thing! A diet should not only be effective and satisfy hunger, it should also be flexible and convenient so it doesn't get in the way of living your ordinary life and doesn't disrupt social interaction. You can add sweets and other tasty but not very useful products to the diet for emotional comfort.

You should pay attention to the importance of meals at the same time, because that helps the body develop a conditioned reflex (digestive juices will be secreted before food is taken), which means the process of digestion and absorption will improve!
