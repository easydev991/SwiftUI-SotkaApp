Day 62. Pros and cons of vegetarianism





With vegetarians more active across the web, and with media and bloggers paying more attention to the topic, it is obvious we could not walk past it in our program either. At the same time we will try to stay as objective and concrete as we can in today's infopost. And we are always ready to discuss!

What is vegetarianism?




Vegetarianism is an approach to eating based on mostly plant food and a refusal of meat. People can choose this approach for different reasons: ethical (pity for poor animals — a very common reason in developed countries such as Canada), environmental (there is a mistaken assumption that meat production is less ecological than plant farming), economic (no money for meat — a very common reason in developing countries such as India), medical (individual intolerance), religious (Buddhism, Hinduism).

How do vegetarians / vegans / raw-foodists differ?


Because the idea of vegetarianism has existed for a great many years (roughly from the mid-19th century), like any other idea it has picked up different interpretations, branches, and modifications over that time. The most popular of them are worth covering separately in today's article.

Veganism — a hard form of vegetarianism that completely rules out eating products of animal origin, fish and seafood, milk, and eggs.

Raw foodism (from the word "raw") — rules out any products of animal origin. The diet is only (or mostly) raw plant food that has not been through any kind of processing (heat, salting, fermenting, pickling, or any other kind of food processing). It has branches in the form of fruitarianism and mono-eating.

Just for a laugh:
Online you can buy a special juicer designed for raw-foodists which, according to the manufacturer, preserves useful minerals, vitamins, and generally everything juice can preserve.

A regular juicer is not suitable for raw-foodists, because raw foodism assumes a refusal of any food processing. But a special raw-foodist juicer — that is welcome.

Criticism of veganism as a marketing model of corporations will be mentioned at the end of the post.


Fruitarianism — the diet is limited to raw plant fruits, for example fruit and berries, plus nuts and seeds.

Mono-eating — in one meal (or over a certain period of time) only one plant product is allowed.

The main vegan arguments against meat


Eating meat leads to cancer

At the moment there is not a single study that shows a direct link between eating products of animal origin (including those rich in so-called "bad" cholesterol) and cancer. All the popular studies (including the China Study) refer to a relationship between meat intake and biomarkers that mark a predisposition to cancer, and those biomarkers only partly depend on diet.

To put the situation in perspective — there is a huge number of studies that directly show a link between tobacco use (smoking or chewing) and cancer.

Vegetarians live longer

A very large and very high-quality long-term study was done in the United Kingdom, and its results were published in 2016. Based on data from 60,000 participants, the scientists concluded that there is no substantial difference in lifespan between vegetarians and ordinary people.

Advantages of vegetarianism


Switching to any version of a vegetarian diet will, for many people, mean giving up familiar foods that very often are not useful or healthy at all. We mean, in particular, fast food, fatty food, sweets, snacks, soda, and so on. Giving that food up will lead to a sharp drop in the overall calorie content of the diet, and also shift the balance between protein, fat, and carbohydrate toward a healthier (read: WHO-recommended) ratio. You have to eat a lot of hypothetical apples or carrots to get the same number of calories as in one hypothetical Mars bar. Roughly, an apple has around 50 calories per 100 grams of product, a bar has 450 — a difference of 9 (!!!) times. So a person who chooses a vegetarian diet starts eating healthier food, and the feeling of lightness that many beginners feel may have nothing to do with giving up meat products.

Another advantage of a diet made of plant food is that it is rich in fiber and antioxidants.

Criticism of vegetarianism


A hard artificial restriction of the diet naturally also creates certain problems for those who have decided to give up meat. In particular, a vegetarian diet rules out the possibility of giving the body the amount of vitamin B12 it needs, which can lead not only to problems with the nervous system, but also to more serious problems (for example, pernicious anemia). Vitamin B12 is the extreme case of how incomplete a vegetarian diet can be, but very often it is also unbalanced in many other parameters for a number of reasons (limited food choice or limited money, and so on). Incomplete nutrition in the long run can lead to serious health problems, so any vegetarian should know about these weaknesses of this approach to eating and compensate for them by eating foods rich in the needed substances.

Even if you follow the mildest of all the "plant" eating schemes described in this infopost — namely vegetarianism — you cannot simply take out products of animal origin without serious damage to yourself and your health. You have to replace them with products of similar nutritional value, which is never (or almost never) mentioned in vegetarian texts.

You can also put insufficient protein supply here — and that is the building material for every cell. Simple math tells us that even to hit the minimum acceptable protein level (0.8 grams per 1 kg of body weight), a person weighing 70 kg will need to eat more than 2 kilograms of fruit and vegetables (at an average protein content of 5–8 grams per 100 grams). And that really can become a problem if we are talking about the extreme versions of vegetarianism, that is veganism or raw foodism. Because a standard vegetarian diet includes various dairy products that are rich in complete protein.

In the same point you can also put a lack of the essential omega-3 fatty acids, namely docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), which you cannot get from plant fatty acids. That is, chia oil or flaxseed oil alone will not cut it here. A deficit of these acids is especially dangerous for children and nursing mothers, because a lack of them in a child's body at an early age can hit all further development extremely hard.

The problems this kind of imbalance in eating can lead to are quite varied, starting from problems with the teeth (they suffer first) and ending with various kinds of cancer, which vegetarians are significantly more prone to than average.

We also have to mention the influential vegan / raw-foodist scenes, which have their own gurus, their own specialists, and even their own special "doctors". In fact this is already a whole culture, and belonging to it is, for some people, the stimulus to switch their eating scheme to vegetarian. And in this case that culture has clear signs of a cult (which is also why it is criticized so often): the presence of adepts initiated into secret knowledge (a split into those inside and those outside), aggressive proselytism (the drive to convert everyone to their faith), psychological pressure, and so on. It is easy for a person to fall into a cult, and much harder to get out.

Still, a fairly recent study showed that 84% of vegetarians eventually go back to eating meat, and they do it first of all because of their health.

In closing it is worth saying that many researchers of vegetarianism's recent popularity tend to tie it to active lobbying of their interests by companies that make so-called "organic" products, including ones with special labeling for vegetarians. In fact they are creating a new sales market for themselves, where thanks to a marketing policy built around a healthy lifestyle and a conscious approach to eating, they can set higher prices on simple products. Which means they get more profit than on the traditional, more competitive markets. Just look at the number of open vegan / vegetarian cafes and other venues, forums, events, and so on. Money, as always, is at the head of everything...
