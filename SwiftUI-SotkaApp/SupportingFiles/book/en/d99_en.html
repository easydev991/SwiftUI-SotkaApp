Day 99. A farewell gift (Memory-Time / Effect-Time graph)





100 days is, after all, a long stretch. And in that time you managed to get really outstanding results! You made workout part of your life, taught yourself to train regularly, figured out nutrition and got answers to all the main questions that will let you keep moving on your own. And that is really great!

We have only one last thing left to do — give you a small farewell gift from the organizers' team.



This graph is our farewell gift. Look at it carefully, because understanding this graph can change your life once and for all. Doubt it? Look at your life from the side, at what you have managed to achieve, at the results you have now. This graph explains why they are exactly what they are. If you like your life, this graph can explain why you like it. If you do not, this graph can explain that too. But, much more importantly, it will show you what you need to do to change your life for the better.

The graph has several parts, and to understand it fully you need to take them one by one. At first it may seem we are telling obvious things, but we recommend reading to the end, because it gets more interesting with every paragraph. We promise! Let's start with the "Memory–Time" curve:



To understand this part of the graph it is enough to try answering a few simple questions. For example, what did you have for breakfast today? Most likely that question will cause no problems (unless you are reading our infoposts before breakfast). And what did you have for breakfast yesterday? That question is a bit harder, but many people will still handle it. And what about remembering what you had for breakfast a week ago? A month ago? A year ago? The farther we go into the past, the smaller the chance you can remember the answer. That is exactly the phenomenon the "Memory–Time" part of the graph shows.

Over time memory fades, and absolutely everyone always faces that. The wise ancient Chinese noticed it and invented the saying "Weak ink is stronger than the best memory." It is hard for anyone to remember exactly the events of even a year ago, not to mention those of 5 or 10 years ago. This is a fairly obvious part of the graph, but we had to tell it.

Now we move to the second part of the graph — the "Effect–Time" curve:



If we try to explain this idea in two sentences: the results you have today are not the results of today's actions and decisions. They are the results of actions you did and decisions you made many years ago. This concept is less popular than the previous one, but it is also simple enough to understand. If you spend 30 minutes on training today, you will not immediately get the body of your dreams, will not instantly fix your health and will not instantly raise your self-esteem. But if you set aside 30 minutes for training every day, over time you can transform yourself in an incredible way. And the more time that passes, the stronger the effect!

Another great example we really like is connected with studying and preparing for exams. When you read a textbook, you do not get results the moment you finish the next page. Of course you learn something new, but that is only a drop of water in the ocean. But when exam time comes (and you have read the textbooks), you can pass it without problems and get an excellent grade. And the grade is only the start of a long chain of consequences. If you study excellently, you have a much better chance of getting a good job, and a good job will bring you a lot of new opportunities.

Finally, our favorite example that illustrates this graph is planting a tree. At the moment you put a seed in the ground, you will not see any tree. You will not even see a small sprout. It will take days, weeks, months for the sown seed to sprout. And for it to grow into a huge tree it will take not even years, but decades. But when you later look at that tree, you will always remember that it appeared thanks to one small seed planted many, many years ago.

By itself, one page of a textbook, just like one push-up by itself, just like one sown seed, will not bring you magical results at the moment of the action. But the cumulative effect over time can change your life in a way you cannot even imagine!

We promised it would get more interesting, right? It gets even better from here!

What happens when the curves cross? Let's go back to the original picture:



Let's look at this graph carefully once more and try to understand what it shows. At point 0 on the "Time" axis we see some action being done today, which we remember perfectly, but which so far gives no results. If we rewind time forward to the end point on the "Time" axis on our graph, say 10 years forward, we will see that although the results (consequences) of the action have become huge, at the same time we will almost not remember the action itself. And here we come right up to why this graph is so good.

For example, it can explain why most people who promise themselves to start training on Monday and get in shape quit after a couple of weeks. They have moved only a little to the right on the "Time" axis from the starting point. They remember perfectly the actions they did, the exhausting workouts, the diets and all the rest, but they still have not gotten any results (or almost none), because too little time has passed. At that moment they decide to quit everything, because it seems to them that their actions give nothing.

Another example this graph tells us about. Surely someone you know is now unhappy with their life, job, family, relationships, health — with anything at all. In fact, their life now is the result of actions done many, many years ago, which they remember almost nothing about. But they remember what happened recently, and we are ready to bet they will look for the cause in their RECENT actions and decisions. Or look for the cause in circumstances outside them: a bad boss, a corrupt government, unfaithful friends, toxic relationships, and so on.

It is worth noting that a similar example works for positive results too. If someone you know finally got the life they always dreamed of, got the longed-for job, met a loving partner, started traveling the world, then in this case they will also look for the cause in their recent actions.

To sum up everything written above: roughly speaking, your life in 5 years is determined by the actions (EVEN THE SMALLEST ONES) you do today. And that is the biggest problem of our generation, because we live in the time of smartphones, social networks and instant reward for action, which makes it harder and harder to focus on long-term prospects, and fewer and fewer people can do it. Just remember this, and everything will be fine!
