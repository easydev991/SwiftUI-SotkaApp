Today is our last information post, because tomorrow the TURBO block starts. There will be interesting things there, but everything in its time.

This is first-hand information, because we stood at the start of workout back in 2009 and watched history being made. At that time a healthy lifestyle was not popular, crumbling outdoor playgrounds in parks and schools stood empty, only a handful of young people trained on bars, and the word "workout" itself did not exist. Nobody could imagine how revolutionary what we were doing would become, or where it would lead.

But let's go in order.

What is workout?


Street Workout (earlier known as WorkOut or Ghetto Workout) is a street subculture that combines a unique approach to training, a drive for well-rounded personal development, and social activity.

As you can see from the definition, workout has three parts. Many people associate workout only with the unique training approach, so we will start there.

They say it is better to see once than to hear a hundred times. The videos that started it all let you answer for yourself how the training side of workout differs from regular PE, CrossFit, or the popular Soviet-era bar work. Look up names like Ghetto Workout 24h Fitness, Barilla and Hannibal, G.I.A.N.T., The Beast aka Lord Vital, and Harlem S.E.A.L.S. & Calisthenics Kingz. Those early clips show the style better than any description.

Until 2006, when videos like that started appearing on YouTube, nobody offered such an approach to training. Of course people already did some exercises and elements on bars (mostly borrowed from gymnastics), but there was no creative approach to exercises and combinations, and none of the rest that Bar-tendaz, Ruff Ryders and other teams brought. Each member of each team had their own unique style. For them training was not only a way to strengthen the body, but also a way to express themselves. Why come to the playground every time and do the same thing? The same exercises, the same reps? Why lock yourself into a program or a plan? If different playgrounds let you do completely different exercises, why not use that? In that sense outdoor training is close in spirit to street dance.

Those videos showed that training outdoors (on sports grounds or any suitable structures) gives great results in health, strength and looks with no money spent, and they inspired a huge number of people around the world to start. We should say again that workout has absolutely NOTHING to do with Soviet gymnastics. In those early videos there are basically no artistic-gymnastics exercises. The people it started with were not gymnasts and were not inspired by gymnasts. They just trained on outdoor playgrounds and shared their experience and knowledge. Then YouTube and social networks appeared, and the speed of spread of these unusual sessions multiplied.

Now it is impossible to find out who exactly started it, but we can say for sure that well-known people like Beast and GIANT were among the first, and a little later came the teams Harlem S.E.A.L.S, Calisthenics Kings, Wingate Family and Bar-Barians. That is about those who became known thanks to the internet, but they also learned from someone. Unfortunately, those names are unknown.

That covers the training side. We will only add that abroad such training is called calisthenics or bodyweight training, and we can move on.

How workout appeared


We mentioned American videos for a reason: the new approach to familiar exercises was born in the United States. Many people wrongly attribute it only to residents of poor neighborhoods. In reality it was not like that. People simply gathered in parks on outdoor playgrounds and trained together. On the playground everyone is equal, and the only thing that matters is that you came to train.

In Russia this new approach became known in the autumn of 2008, when several guys from the team One More Day found the videos mentioned above and were just as amazed as you. They caught the spirit of outdoor training, decided it was insanely cool, and decided not only to start themselves, but to introduce as many people as possible to it. The guys themselves did parkour, so they decided to invent a name for the new direction as well. After some thought they created a community called Ghetto Workout (after one of the videos) and started holding weekly Sunday open free sessions for anyone who wanted. The idea was to show people that you can become stronger, healthier and better looking through training that is available to everyone, using imagination and bodyweight as resistance.

Unlike the American team Bar-tendaz, which also held master classes, trainings and lectures about a healthy lifestyle, but did it for money, the guys from One More Day did everything completely free and on pure enthusiasm. They also wanted to show people in other cities that to develop workout, organize and run sessions, you need nothing at all. It is enough to create a community, gather friends and start training. Many cities started following their example and organizing their own workout communities. Very quickly workout became popular among young people not only across Russia, but also in neighboring countries. Around the same time a certain philosophy of this direction and the spirit of workout as a street subculture started to take shape.

Thanks to its availability, interest and effectiveness, only a couple of years later Ghetto Workout and outdoor training became hugely popular among school and university students, and Russia became the world leader in this direction, far ahead of other countries both in the number of people training and in the level of infrastructure. It all started with four people and their friends. Gradually the number of people training grew, and completely new people who had learned about workout online started coming to the open sessions. At the peak of popularity the number of workout athletes in Russia exceeded 100,000!!!

To be fair, in all that time nothing changed in the United States, and outdoor training stayed at the same popularity level. It is still a local story of a small group of people who gather in parks after work or on weekends.

What you need to know about workout


To finish today's infopost, we want you to remember these main points:

Reminder:
1. The unusual training was invented in the States.

2. Workout (both the word itself and the direction as we know it) was invented in Russia.

3. The training side of workout has nothing to do with artistic gymnastics, Soviet or modern.

4. Anyone can train on bars, regardless of sex, age or fitness level.

5. Workout is not a sport and not extreme.

6. There are no coaches in workout.

7. Workout is the street.

8. Workout is always free.


Now a bit more about the last four points.

Workout is not a sport and not extreme. Why is it not a sport? Because the main attribute of any sport is athletes standing against each other, comparing achievements, competing. Workout traditionally rejects the competitive component, because the main goal is not wins, records or medals, but the health and development of the person training. That view is laid out in more detail in the article "Street fitness or professional sport?"

Why is it not extreme? Because extreme is aimed at thrills, risk and an adrenaline spike. The task of workout is to improve a person without danger to life and health. That is why this direction is built on basic, natural exercises, but with imagination you can refine them and create new versions and combinations.

Unfortunately, many people get a wrong picture of workout from countless internet clips and TV reports. In those videos the word "workout" is used only in the title to attract attention, and what they actually show are gymnastic, acrobatic and any other spectacular/dangerous/pretty tricks on bars that have nothing to do with workout culture.

Even sadder, there are now guys who watch all those trendy trick videos with huge view counts and start treating workout the same way. They do not see training in it, they do not see a way to develop body and spirit. All they see is a chance to surprise others or show off. Usually they train for a year or two, reach a certain level and quit. Because they no longer have time to hang on the bars for 5 hours a day (usually they were still in school or university when they started), because other interests appear, or because they got injured trying to learn a hard skill without proper preparation. We have seen this more than once, in many cities. What is notable is that the people we started with in 2009 still train. Yes, they may rarely come to the Sunday group sessions, because they have little time because of family and work, but in their own yard they still train regularly. For them workout is not just another hobby. It is part of life.

The next point is dedicated to the growing number of pseudo-coaches in workout who come from fitness or gymnastics, or just slick guys who try to use a trendy word to enrich themselves, although they have no idea what workout is. Such people and their behavior have an EXTREMELY negative effect on the image of workout in the eyes of regular people.

There are no coaches in workout. From the very first days the whole culture was built on the principle "each one teaches each one."

Historical note:
This is an African-American saying that appeared in the USA during the active slave trade, when Black slaves had no access to education at all (including basic reading skills). If one of the slaves managed to learn to read (or if their owner taught them), they considered it their duty to teach someone else. FOR FREE.


In workout culture there are no coaches or teachers as such. Everyone who comes to the playground is equal, but anyone is ready to share their experience and help others. Workout turns playgrounds into gathering places for like-minded people who come after work or study not only to train, but also to talk, learn something themselves and share something with others. Only that way can you reach the main goal of workout — that as many people as possible learn about this direction and start doing it!

The meaning of the training side of workout is exactly that ORDINARY people train, and PROFESSIONALS do not. That is the secret of its appeal. If a colleague from work managed to get a serious result, that will motivate you much more than a picture of a pumped fitness model on a magazine cover who dedicated a whole life, a pile of money and their health to a pretty picture.

Workout is the street. This direction was born on outdoor sports grounds. Unlike fitness clubs, gyms or gymnastics halls, playgrounds are open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and they are completely free. You can come and train at any convenient time, for as long as you want. Besides, the best example is a personal example, and in that sense, when we train outdoors, we set an example for others. A positive example that can push someone else to come out to the playground and start changing their life for the better.

That is how it is. A lot of text today, but we decided it would be interesting to learn how it all started and where you can go after finishing SOTKA.
