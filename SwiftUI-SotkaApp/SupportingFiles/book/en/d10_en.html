Day 10. Exercise tempo




When people tell us that bodyweight exercises aren't enough to get into great shape, because the set is so limited, we can't help smiling.

Besides the huge number of variations of basic exercises and combinations you can invent, there's a whole range of other factors you can change to give yourself extra load and adapt your training. And today we'll talk about exactly one of those factors: exercise tempo!

Tempo


Tempo is about the speed of the exercise, and the exercise itself is most often split into 3 or 4 phases (depending on whether the starting position is included or not):

1. Starting position.
2. Concentric phase (the muscle shortens).
3. End position.
4. Eccentric phase (the muscle lengthens).

For example, a tempo of 0-1-0-3 means you contract the muscles in 1 second (explosive strength), hold 0 seconds at the end point, lengthen the muscles in 3 seconds (slowly), and give 0 seconds of rest before the next rep in the set.

The same exercise can be completely different in difficulty if you change the tempo — but how do you know what speed to use?

Choosing tempo


First, it all depends on your goals. If your job is to do a large number of reps, you need to choose a high speed. The less time you give yourself to do the exercise, the more you train explosive strength. Also, explosive tempo is most often used not for simple push-up variations, but for plyometric ones — where there are elements of pushing off the ground, claps, jumps, and so on — to maximally recruit the fast muscle fibers. If your goal is to build muscle, the exercise tempo should be slower, and the time of the eccentric phase should be longer than the concentric (for example, concentric — 2 seconds, then eccentric — 4). The longer the muscles are under load, the more effective the exercise becomes!

Second, the speed of the exercise depends on your level and on how safe your technique is. There's no point in doing it fast and sloppy (using momentum or wriggling), because that way not only won't give the result you want — it can also lead to injuries. So first you need to learn safe technique and gain confidence in the movements, and only then can you think about changing tempo.

Third, you should also take into account the tempo you usually train at. We've said more than once that over time your body gets used to the load you give it and learns to do it with minimal effort. That's good for the body, but bad for reaching goals. That's exactly why you need to make changes to your training from time to time! If you usually train at a slow tempo, try a medium or fast one and vice versa, or try adding a pause in the end position of the exercise, and so on. Experiment and don't let your body adapt!

Fast and slow tempo


It's also worth noting that a fast tempo, or even an explosive tempo, can be very hard in terms of correct technical execution, and the limit can be not only the muscles but the nervous system (a clear example is playing musical instruments, or fake-out moves in team sports, which are easy to break down into movements but hard to do quickly and technically correctly). And a slow tempo, on the contrary, trains endurance, including of the nervous system, which has to send a nerve impulse of constant strength to the muscles for a long time. So for full development (and maybe for breaking a training plateau), changing exercise tempo will have a positive effect.

Super-slow reps have a lot of advantages that make them an effective method of building muscle mass. This method minimizes momentum in order to maximize the force applied to the muscle being trained. It will help form a brain–muscle connection, because slow movement forces you to concentrate on the contraction. Fatigue builds up gradually in all the muscle fibers involved in the movement, which lowers the injury risk if the exercise is done incorrectly. The load on the joints also decreases.

The ego problem


Very often a lot of people (especially beginners) refuse a slow tempo because they worry too much about what people around them will think.

For example, they can do 5 pull-ups in 30 seconds, going up slowly and coming down slowly, or they can do 20–25 pull-ups in the same time at max tempo. No doubt the second option will impress people watching from the side much more. But we don't come to the playground to wow someone with our workout, right? We come to train, to become healthier, stronger, and better-looking! So during training let's think only about our own session and our own goals, and do only what will take us closer to the result we want!
