Day 12. What's the best time to train?





In the modern world we're constantly surrounded by various worries and problems, so it often becomes extremely hard to find time for training. Street Workout became a way out of that situation for many people, because it let us remove all the side elements of the training process that cost time (for example, you don't need to go anywhere — you can train at home or at the nearest outdoor sports playground), while keeping the effectiveness and results.

Still, the question of choosing a time to train remains relevant, and today we'll talk about the factors that affect it. We should say right away that there is no single universal best time to train; this question, like most others, needs to be approached individually.

The most important factor to take into account is the ability to train regularly at the same time. If training is much more effective than not training, then regular training is much more effective than training now and then. So try to find that window in your daily schedule when you can always (or almost always) train. It can be morning, it can be afternoon, it can be evening, it can even be night. In any case it's best to pick one time and regularly train at that time.

Besides that there's a whole range of secondary factors that affect the choice of training time:

Meals. Training on an empty stomach, just like on a full one, will be fairly hard. So it's best to train some time after eating. How long exactly is an individual question, but for an average person the recommendation to wait two to four hours after a meal, with a light snack (an apple, for example) an hour before training, will work.

The cardiovascular system (CVS) and other body systems. Active training is a load not only on the muscles, but also on the CVS and the respiratory system. When the body is coming out of sleep, all its systems are working at lower revs, so to speak. So if you want to train in the first half of the day, you should give enough time to warm-up and getting warm. You don't race a car in winter without warming it up, right? Same with your body. With a normal daily schedule, closer to sleep the body starts slowing all its systems on its own, calms down, and prepares for rest. That's why it's recommended to train at least a couple of hours before sleep, so you don't disrupt natural biorhythms.

Results. However banal it may seem, results are not the last item on our list of factors. If you always train in the morning but don't see any improvements, try training at another time of day. It's quite possible that morning sessions just aren't for you.

Support. It's much easier to find friends for a shared workout in the afternoon and evening than in the morning. And training together is much more effective than training alone!

Goals. If the goal is to gain muscle mass, it's best to train in the evening, a couple of hours before sleep. As we all know, training leads to microtears in the muscles, which recover during rest, making the muscles stronger. So after a good workout you need to eat and sleep, and that opportunity is only there in the evening.

Work. If the job involves physical labor, a morning workout is not the best decision. For it to be effective you need to go 100%, and that's hard to do knowing a whole day of hard work is still ahead! Then again, when you come home tired after work, you probably won't want to train either. So here you have to look for yourself.

Finally we want to remind you of a well-known saying: those who want it look for opportunities, those who don't look for excuses!
